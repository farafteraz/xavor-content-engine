## Executive Summary

The May campaign promised something broad: **From Data to Outcomes** — turn your data into business results. June's signals let us get specific. Every major platform — Salesforce, ServiceNow, Oracle — shipped AI agents to general availability in the same month. Out-of-the-box agents now promise go-lives in weeks, even days. That changes the buyer's question.

The question is no longer *whether* to use AI agents. It is now two sharper, more practical questions:

> **What do we build versus buy — and how fast can we actually get to production without creating lock-in, cost, or compliance problems we'll regret?**

This is the July sub-angle. It keeps the "From Data to Outcomes" lineage but narrows it to the decision every enterprise is making right now:

> **The AI-First Enterprise: Build, Buy, Ship Faster.** Xavor helps enterprises make the right build-versus-buy calls and reach governed production faster than they could alone.

It also retires the tired "pilots to production" framing while keeping the substance. The new spin: **speed to production is the competitive metric of 2026** — but the speed vendors advertise (six clicks, five weeks) is not the speed most enterprises actually get. The gap between a working demo and a dependable system is where build-versus-buy decisions, integration, governance, and cost discipline live. That gap is exactly what Xavor closes.

The stakes are real: the digest cites Gartner projecting that **over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by 2027** — most of them from buying the wrong thing, building what should have been bought, or stalling on the way to production.

## Strategic Thesis

After June's GA wave, enterprise buyers are asking:

- Which agents should we buy off the shelf, and which workflows are too proprietary to outsource?
- The vendor says five-week go-live — what does it actually take for *us* to get there safely?
- How do we move fast without locking ourselves into one vendor's ecosystem?
- If we build, do we have the engineering capacity? If we buy, can we integrate and govern it?
- How do we hit speed targets without blowing up cost or failing an audit?

There is a pattern emerging in the market, captured in the digest: **front-end, standardized, customer-facing agents are being bought; back-end, proprietary workflows are being built.** Xavor's position is to be the partner who helps enterprises make that call correctly — and then ship, integrate, and govern the result faster than going it alone.

**Narrative:** speed to production is the new advantage, but it's earned through smart build-versus-buy decisions and disciplined execution — not bought in a click.

## Why This Matters Now

The June Sentinel signals define a tight Q3 window where the build-versus-buy and speed questions are live and urgent:

- **Salesforce** shipped Summer '26 (GA June 15) — multi-agent orchestration, a 50+ agent IT Service Domain Pack, GA MCP servers — and acquired Fin (formerly Intercom) for ~$3.6B, plus the pay-per-resolution Agentforce Help Agent. The digest notes some customers going live in as little as five weeks.
- **ServiceNow** brought IT AI Specialists to GA in June and opened Action Fabric to external agents, raising an immediate build-vs-configure decision for every ITSM team.
- **Oracle** moved Oracle Integration into its "Age of AI" (June 18) with agentic workflows and projects-as-MCP-servers — buy the agents, build the integration.
- **Build-vs-buy tension is explicit in the market:** practitioners report front-end tools being bought and back-end proprietary workflows being built, while vendor lock-in fear ("agent framework capture") is rising.
- **The downside is quantified:** Gartner reportedly expects 40%+ of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by 2027, and a large share of pilots never reach production at all.

Conclusion: **build-versus-buy clarity and speed-to-production discipline are the Q3 play — and the platform wave makes July the moment to publish on it.**

## Risks and Guardrails

| Risk | Guardrail |
|---|---|
| Generic AI messaging | Every asset must tie to a build-vs-buy decision, a speed-to-production blocker, a platform, a cost line, or a measurable outcome. |
| "Speed" read as recklessness | Always pair speed with governance, cost attribution, and lock-in avoidance — fast *and* safe, never fast at any cost. |
| Platform-only messaging | Don't treat Summer '26, IT AI Specialists, or Oracle's Age of AI as isolated product news — frame each as a build-vs-buy and speed decision. |
| Over-reliance on the August 2 deadline | Anchor compliance messaging on operational risk too; the Omnibus deferral softened some high-risk timelines. |
| Unverified statistics | Confirm every figure against the primary source before publishing; see the data-confidence note. Apply this to the Gartner 40% cancellation and "front-end buy / back-end build" claims especially. |
| Conflated "79% governance gap" | Keep the Writer adoption-struggle stat (79%) separate from Deloitte's governance-maturity stat (21%). |
| Inconsistent Agentforce ARR | Do not state a single Agentforce ARR figure as fact; the digest reports both "$800M / 169%" and "$1.2B / 205%." |
