# Post 13 — 2026-09-22 — carousel
**Angle:** Before an agent goes live it needs an owner, a decision boundary, an escalation path, and a 5.1-month median payback in view, or it's exposure wearing a production label.

## Caption
Most agent pilots never reach production, and the ones that do are often shipped without an owner or a payback number attached. Here is the pre-launch bar we set for every agent before it touches a real workflow.

## Copy

**Slide 1:**
88% of agent pilots die before production. The survivors get shipped with no owner, no boundary, no number. A production label on that is exposure.

**Slide 2:**
An owner. One named person accountable for the agent's behavior in production, its escalations, and its results. Not the team. A person.

**Slide 3:**
A decision boundary. The exact actions the agent may take alone, and where it must stop and hand off. Written down before launch.

**Slide 4:**
An escalation path. When the agent hits its boundary, who catches it and how fast. And a shutdown you have tested. 35% of executives can't stop a rogue agent today.

**Slide 5:**
A payback number. Median agent payback runs 5.1 months. Name the metric and the date it clears, or you can't defend the spend.

**Slide 6:**
Set the pre-launch bar for every agent now. Owner, boundary, escalation, payback: we build all four into the agent before it ships. Get in touch.

## Evidence used
- [E16]: 88% of agent pilots fail to reach production (Slide 1); 5.1-month median payback (Slide 5).
- [E44]: every production agent needs a defined owner, decision boundary, escalation path, and success metric before launch — the four-part standard structuring the whole carousel.
- [E20]: 35% couldn't immediately shut down a rogue agent (Slide 4).

## Design note
Number the four requirements 1 through 4 across slides 2–5 as a running counter in a corner, so the reader feels a checklist completing. Keep type large and quiet, one requirement per slide, no icons.
