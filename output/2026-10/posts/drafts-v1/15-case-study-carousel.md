# Post 15 — 2026-10-28 — case-study carousel
**Angle:** A single posture configured across the Harness, the Control Tower, and Unity Catalog in a regulated estate proves the cross-platform seam is closable, and that closing it is delivery work, not a product you can buy.

## Caption
Every platform governs its own estate well. None governs its neighbor. In a regulated client's stack, we set one control posture across the Salesforce Harness, ServiceNow's Control Tower, and Databricks Unity Catalog. Here is what that took, and why no vendor ships it.

## Copy

**Slide 1:**
Three vendors each promised governed AI. Each stopped at its own edge. The gap between them was where the audit risk lived. We closed it.

**Slide 2:**
The estate ran agents on three platforms. Salesforce's Trusted Enterprise AI Harness governed its agents. ServiceNow's AI Control Tower governed its own. Unity Catalog logged every Databricks tool call. Each honest. None aware of the others.

**Slide 3:**
A policy that holds on one platform means little when an agent crosses into the next. An attached Source in Databricks says nothing about what the Harness allows in Salesforce. The seam is where accountability disappears.

**Slide 4:**
So we configured all three to one standard. Same access rules, same logging expectations, same human-oversight points, written once and mapped into the Harness, the Control Tower, and Unity Catalog's RBAC defaults.

**Slide 5:**
No vendor sells this posture, because it lives between their products. It is integration work in a regulated estate: thirty years of that discipline, applied by one hand configuring every platform to the same line.
