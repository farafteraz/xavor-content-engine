# Post 12 — 2026-08-24 — carousel
**Angle:** Once pricing is $2 per resolution, ROI stops being a cost question and becomes a resolution-rate question, and the gap between Fin's 76% and Agentforce's 62% is rate engineering you have to sustain.

## Caption
Pay-per-resolution pricing kills the cost objection to support agents. It quietly hands you a harder one: hold the resolution rate, or the math never closes. Here's what actually moves that number.

## Copy

Slide 1:
Agentforce charges $2 per resolved issue. No resolution, no charge. The ROI question is no longer cost. It's the rate you can hold.

Slide 2:
On help.salesforce.com, Agentforce handled 4.3 million inquiries and resolved 70% autonomously. That 70% is the business case. Every point lands on the invoice.

Slide 3:
Salesforce is buying Fin for about $3.6 billion. Fin resolves roughly 76% of support volume in some deployments. Agentforce, roughly 62% case resolution. The price is fixed. The rate is not.

Slide 4:
At $2 per resolution, the difference between 62% and 76% is not a feature gap. It's revenue you either capture or leave sitting in the human queue at full cost.

Slide 5:
Resolution rate is engineering, not procurement. Retrieval quality, escalation rules, the data the agent reads before it answers. That work is yours to build and yours to sustain.

Slide 6:
Turn resolution rate into a number you can hold, then raise it. Get in touch.

## Evidence used
- [E19]: flat $2 per autonomously resolved issue, no charge on human handoff, negative feedback, or abandonment — Slides 1 and 4.
- [E20]: help.salesforce.com reference deployment, 4.3M inquiries, 70% resolved autonomously — Slide 2.
- [E23]: Salesforce acquiring Fin for ~$3.6B; Fin ~76% resolution in some deployments vs. Agentforce ~62% case resolution — Slides 3 and 4.

## Design note
Keep one number dominant per slide (the $2, the 70%, the 76% vs 62%) in a large numeral, body copy small beneath it. Carry a single accent color through the numbers so the rate figures read as one continuous argument across slides.
