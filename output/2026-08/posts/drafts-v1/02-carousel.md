# Post 2 — 2026-08-04 — carousel
**Angle:** 90% of enterprises fund AI governance, 74% believe they'd pass an audit, only 27% are mature enough to prove it, and the platform embeds high-risk AI without classifying or evidencing it for you.

## Caption
As of August 2, high-risk AI under the EU AI Act is enforceable, and the gap that matters isn't whether you funded governance. It's whether you can prove the controls exist when an auditor asks. Most enterprises have funded one capability and assumed they bought the other.

## Copy

Slide 1:
90% of enterprises fund AI governance. 74% believe they'd pass an audit. 27% are mature enough to prove it. Those are three different numbers for a reason.

Slide 2:
Funding a governance line item buys you a budget. Passing a control-mapping audit buys you evidence. As of August 2, only the second one is legally material. (Schellman, n=525)

Slide 3:
The platform embeds high-risk AI into employment, credit, and education workflows. It does not classify that AI as high-risk for you, and it does not produce the audit trail. That work stays on your side of the line.

Slide 4:
This is where the 74% comes from. Confidence built on a funded budget, not on controls anyone has mapped to a specific system a regulator can name.

Slide 5:
Fines run to €15M or 3% of annual turnover. The question an auditor asks is narrow: show me the control for this system. A budget is not an answer.

Slide 6:
Close the gap between funding governance and demonstrating it. Book a readiness sprint now. Get in touch.
