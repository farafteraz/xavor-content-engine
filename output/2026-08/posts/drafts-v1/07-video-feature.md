# Post 7 — 2026-08-13 — video feature
**Angle:** BMW put Figure robots into just-in-sequence logistics, but the robot is the platform. The edge AI, MES connectivity, and OT/IT pipelines that make it deploy are the integration layer under it.

## Caption
BMW is running humanoids in production logistics at Spartanburg. The robot ships from Figure. The part that connects it to your MES, your OT network, and your line sequencing is the part nobody sells you.

## Copy

**Beat 1 — 0:00–0:10**
We see: A Figure 03 lifting a bin on a Spartanburg-style line, then a hard cut to a Xavor engineer at a workstation with a factory network diagram on screen.
VO: "BMW is running Figure robots in just-in-sequence logistics. The pilot took ten months before a single robot moved production parts."

**Beat 2 — 0:10–0:22**
We see: The robot from behind, then the camera pulls to reveal the cell around it: conveyors, scanners, a controls cabinet.
VO: "Figure built the humanoid. Figure did not build the connection between that humanoid and the plant it works in. That connection is a project, and it lands on your engineers."

**Beat 3 — 0:22–0:35**
We see: Three labeled panels appear one at a time over a live shot of the line: edge inference, MES handshake, OT/IT boundary.
VO: "Three things decide whether a robot deploys or stalls. Inference that runs at the edge, on the line, without a round trip to the cloud. A clean handshake with the MES so the robot knows what sequence to build. And a controlled path across the OT and IT boundary, so the plant network stays safe."

**Beat 4 — 0:35–0:48**
We see: Navi, Xavor's eldercare robot, operating in a test environment. Cut to Xavor engineers reviewing sensor logs.
VO: "We build robots too. Navi taught us that the hard part is rarely the machine. It is the pipeline underneath: perception, real-time control, and the systems the robot has to talk to without failing quietly."

**Beat 5 — 0:48–1:05**
We see: A digital twin of a work cell running beside the physical cell, values updating in sync.
VO: "A digital twin catches the failures before the line does. You test the sequence, the handoff, and the fault recovery in software first. Then the robot on the floor does what it already did a thousand times in simulation."

**Beat 6 — 1:05–1:20**
We see: Wide shot of a working line, robot and humans in frame, then the Xavor mark.
VO: "The vendors will keep shipping better robots. The layer that turns one into a working station on your floor is yours to build, or ours to build with you. Put a physical AI integration blueprint under your factory floor. Start now."
