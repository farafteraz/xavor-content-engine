# Post 10 — 2026-08-20 — explainer reel
**Angle:** Pay-per-resolution removes the cost objection and installs an operational one. At $2 per resolved issue, the pricing only pays when your engineers sustain a 70% autonomous resolution rate, and holding that rate is work you own.

## Caption
Agentforce charges $2 per resolved issue and nothing when it fails. That looks like risk moved off your books. It moves the risk onto your resolution rate, and holding that rate is engineering, not procurement.

## Copy
Frame 1: Agentforce charges $2 for every issue its agent resolves on its own. Nothing when a customer asks for a human, gives negative feedback, or walks away.

Frame 2: The cost objection is gone. You only pay for outcomes. So the finance conversation ends and a harder one starts.

Frame 3: On Salesforce's own help site, the agent handled 4.3 million inquiries and resolved 70% autonomously.

Frame 4: Read the model closely. $2 pays off at a high resolution rate. Let the rate drop and you keep paying humans for the other half.

Frame 5: The 70% is not a setting you buy. It comes from retrieval quality, clean knowledge, and how well the agent maps to your real cases.

Frame 6: That rate moves with every product change, every new SKU, every edited help article. It decays unless someone owns it.

Frame 7: Salesforce sets the price. You sustain the number underneath it. That number is engineering, and it is yours to hold.

Frame 8: Hit and hold the resolution rate your pricing depends on. Talk to us now.

- [E20]: the help.salesforce.com reference deployment, 4.3M inquiries, 70% resolved autonomously (Frame 3).
