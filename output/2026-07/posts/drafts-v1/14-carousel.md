# Post 14 — 2026-07-23 — carousel
**Angle:** Organizations with fully integrated AI are nearly 4x more likely to report AI-driven revenue growth than those still piloting.

## Caption
The gap between a pilot and revenue isn't a better model. It's whether the AI is wired into how the business actually runs. Grant Thornton found integrated shops report AI revenue growth at 58% against 15% for the ones still experimenting.

## Copy
Slide 1:
58% of companies with fully integrated AI report AI-driven revenue growth. Among the ones still piloting, it's 15%. That's nearly four times the odds (Grant Thornton, 2026).

Slide 2:
The math is easy to misread. Most transformation leads assume the winners simply bought better models. They didn't. They put the model where the work happens.

Slide 3:
A pilot lives in a sandbox. It has no live data, no downstream system to act on, no one accountable for the outcome. It can be impressive and still change nothing.

Slide 4:
Integration is the unglamorous part: connecting the model to real data, giving it permission to act inside a workflow, and measuring what it moves. McKinsey found nearly two-thirds of enterprises experimented, fewer than 10% scaled to value.

Slide 5:
So the revenue question isn't which agent to pilot next. It's whether you can operate the ones you already have. That work is engineering and governance, not another demo.

Slide 6:
