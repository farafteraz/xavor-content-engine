# Post 4 — 2026-07-07 — static
**Angle:** The EU high-risk deadline moved out to December 2027 while operational governance risk got worse, so the urgency is real but it is not regulatory.

## Caption
Brussels gave you 18 more months on high-risk systems. Your agents did not slow down to match. If the compliance calendar is what sets your governance pace, you are pacing off the wrong clock.

## Copy

**Image concept:** Two clocks side by side against a clean off-white field. The left clock is labeled "Compliance" and its hands are being wound backward, with a small tag reading "high-risk deadline: Dec 2027." The right clock is labeled "Operational risk" and runs fast, hands blurred forward, tagged "78% could not pass an audit in 90 days." A thin vertical rule separates them so the eye reads the two speeds against each other.

**On-image text (kicker):**
The clock that moved is not the clock that matters.

**Caption block (below the image, if the layout carries copy):**
The EU AI Act Omnibus pushed the high-risk deadlines back. Annex III systems (recruitment, credit scoring, law enforcement) now have until December 2, 2027. That bought calendar room, not operational room. Ask a harder question: could you pass an independent governance audit in the next 90 days? For 78% of executives, the honest answer is no. The deadline that slipped is regulatory. The one that is closing on you is operational, and no political agreement moves it.

Reset your governance clock now. Get in touch.

## Evidence used
- [E54]: the Omnibus political agreement deferring Annex III high-risk deadlines to December 2, 2027, used to establish the "clock that moved."
- [E20]: Grant Thornton finding that 78% of executives lack strong confidence they could pass an independent AI governance audit within 90 days, used as the operational risk that got worse.

## Design note
Keep the palette quiet and editorial, two clocks and a single dividing rule, nothing else competing. The only visual tension is speed: wind the compliance clock back, blur the operational one forward, so the reader feels the mismatch before they read a word.
