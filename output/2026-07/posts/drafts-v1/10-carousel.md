# Post 10 — 2026-07-16 — carousel
**Angle:** 78% of executives lack confidence they could pass an independent AI governance audit within 90 days, which is an operational readiness gap, not a paperwork gap.

## Caption
Give an independent auditor 90 days and full access to your AI systems. Would you pass? Most executives quietly answer no, and the reason isn't missing documents. It's that no one can fully explain or account for what already runs in production.

## Copy

Slide 1:
78% of executives can't say with confidence they'd pass an independent AI governance audit within 90 days. The problem sits below the paperwork.

Slide 2:
An audit doesn't ask for a policy binder. It asks who approved this agent, what data it touches, and what it did last Tuesday at 2pm.

Slide 3:
Most enterprises can't answer that. Only 21% have mature governance for autonomous agents. The systems shipped faster than the ability to explain them.

Slide 4:
So the 90-day question isn't about compliance writing. It's whether your agents produce a trail an outsider could follow: decisions, data access, corrections, owners.

Slide 5:
That trail is engineering. Logging, access boundaries, evaluation records, a named owner per agent. Build it before the auditor asks, not during the 90 days.

Slide 6:
Close your 90-day audit gap now. Get in touch.

## Evidence used
- [E20]: opening stat, the 78% who lack confidence in passing an independent audit within 90 days (Grant Thornton 2026, n=950).
- [E51]: the 21% with mature governance for autonomous agents, used to show the readiness gap is operational (Deloitte 2026).

## Design note
Clean type-driven slides, one dark accent color, generous whitespace. Slide 1 sets the 78% figure large as the anchor; carry a thin progress line across slides so the reader feels the argument tightening toward the CTA.
