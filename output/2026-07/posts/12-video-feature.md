# Post 12 — 2026-07-21 — video feature
**Angle:** Multi-platform independence is what makes the sequencing decision possible, because the choice of what to automate first sits above any single vendor.

## Caption
Four platforms shipped autonomous agents inside one month. The hard question isn't which one to buy. It's what your company automates first, and that answer never lives inside a single vendor's console.

## Copy

**Video feature — 75-second treatment**

**Beat 1 (0:00–0:08)**
What we see: An engineer at a wide desk, four browser windows open across two monitors: Salesforce, ServiceNow, Oracle Integration, Databricks. No stock-footage gloss. Real tabs, real dashboards.
Spoken: "In June, four platforms we build on shipped autonomous agents to general availability. Same month."

**Beat 2 (0:08–0:20)**
What we see: Quick cuts to each console. Salesforce Summer '26 orchestration screen. ServiceNow IT AI Specialists. Databricks Lakeflow Designer with the Unity Catalog governance panel visible.
Spoken: "Salesforce added multi-agent orchestration on June 15. ServiceNow's IT specialists hit GA the same month. Databricks shipped visual pipelines governed by Unity Catalog. When capability shows up everywhere at once, buying it stops being the decision."

**Beat 3 (0:20–0:33)**
What we see: The engineer turns from the screens to a whiteboard. On it, not an architecture diagram, but a numbered list: support first, then finance close, then field data. She taps the order.
Spoken: "The decision moves up a layer. What do you automate first, and in what order. That question sits above every one of these platforms."

**Beat 4 (0:33–0:47)**
What we see: A second engineer joins. They're tracing a workflow that crosses two of the systems: a case resolved in one platform triggers a pipeline in another. The line runs between vendors, not inside one.
Spoken: "Sequencing is a cross-platform judgment. Where the clean data lives. Which process fails safe. What the first agent hands to the second. You can only answer that if you work across all of them."

**Beat 5 (0:47–0:60)**
What we see: Close on the whiteboard order again. Someone crosses out step two and moves it below step three. A working decision, made in the room.
Spoken: "A partner tied to one platform will tell you to start where that platform is strongest. That's not a sequencing decision. That's a sales map."

**Beat 6 (0:60–0:75)**
What we see: The room from the door. Both engineers, four screens, one whiteboard. Xavor mark appears clean at the end.
Spoken: "We've built on all four for years. So we can tell you the order that's right for your business, not the order that's right for a license. Sequence across your platforms now. Get in touch."

## Evidence used
- [E2]: Salesforce Summer '26 multi-agent orchestration GA June 15, 2026 — stated as the June date and capability.
- [E7]: ServiceNow IT AI Specialists reached GA June 2026 — stated as same-month GA.
- [E10]: Databricks Lakeflow Designer GA, visual pipelines governed by Unity Catalog — stated as the pipeline and governance detail.

## Design note
Shoot in a real working room, not a set: actual consoles on screen, a whiteboard with a legible handwritten sequence the presenter physically reorders on camera. Keep the color neutral and the cuts unhurried so the four-window opening reads as fact, not montage.
